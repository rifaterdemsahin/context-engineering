Context Engineering for Dummies

A complete 8-scene visual storyboard guide explaining why context engineering matters more than prompt engineering, featuring interactive image slots for your generated graphics.

SCENE 01 Core Foundation

The Amnesiac Genius

Storyboard Visual A sleek office. An employee with sharp, cybernetic eyes sits behind a completely clean desk. The manager slaps down a single sticky note. The worker smiles warmly: "Good morning! Who are you, and what do I do?"

Imagine hiring a brilliant employee every morning. He reasons swiftly, parses nuance, and codes flawlessly. But every midnight, his memory wipes completely. Everything he knows in the morning is strictly what sits on his desk when he sits down. That is your AI model. What you place on that desk is Context Engineering.

Key Principle LLMs possess high reasoning but zero persistent state between calls. Intelligence doesn't matter if the workspace is blank or disorganized.
SCENE 02 Strategy Shift

Prompting vs. Context Engineering

Storyboard Visual Split comparison: On the left, a manager obsessively rewrites a note in gold calligraphy while an avalanche of random binders buries the desk. On the right, the manager organizes exactly three clean folders and a sharp pencil.

For years, developers obsessed over prompt engineering—tweaking adjectives, removing ambiguity, and polishing phrasing. But even a Shakespearean note fails if the desk has the wrong manuals. Context engineering is the holistic system design of curating everything on that desk.

Key Principle Prompt engineering is writing the note. Context engineering is assembling the whole desk: System Note, Documents, Memory, and Tools.
SCENE 03 Desk Item 1

The Task Note: Principles vs. Mazes

Storyboard Visual Top: An agent suffocating in a 50-step if/else flowchart taped across the wall. Bottom: The agent calmly glancing at a concise placard: "Approve refunds under 14 days. Escalate if >$100. Use judgment."

Rigid decision trees break on unexpected edge cases. Over-specifying rules causes agents to act like brittle automata or get trapped in contradictions. Setting clear principles and firm boundaries unlocks the model's inherent common sense without risking out-of-bounds behavior.

Key Principle Avoid the instruction maze. Frame roles with concise principles + hard guardrails rather than 50 lines of nested conditional logic.
SCENE 04 Desk Item 2

The Right Documents: Precision RAG

Storyboard Visual Left: A forklift dumps a 4,000-page monorepo binder onto the worker. Right: A funnel where a large net catches 50 candidates, a magnifying glass reranks them to 5 sharp cards, delivering only the exact 12 lines needed.

The most expensive mistake is dumping whole files when 12 lines were needed. Context engineering uses a two-pass system: high-recall vector search (top_k=50) followed by high-precision reranking (top 5), or Just-In-Time file reads via tools.

Key Principle candidates = search(k=50) → rerank(candidates)[:5]. Cast wide for recall, rerank for precision. Keep the desk clear of stale indices.
SCENE 05 Desk Item 3

The Notebook in the Drawer

Storyboard Visual Top: 200 bloated message receipts piling up, stealing attention. Bottom: Old receipts fed into a mini-compressor spitting out a summary card, while a notebook labeled project_notes.md is stored safely inside a desk drawer.

Never let conversation history grow infinitely—old turns distract the model and inflate token bills. Compact older dialogue into rolling summaries. For multi-session memories (user preferences, conventions), keep a file in the drawer and load it only on demand.

Key Principle Short-term memory = Compaction (keep last 10 turns + summary). Long-term memory = External notebook files outside the active context.
SCENE 06 Desk Item 4

Tools & Self-Correcting Errors

Storyboard Visual Left: 5 nearly identical staplers (find_user, lookup_user, search_users) causing decision paralysis. Right: One unified tool get_user(id) with a popup sign: "Missing ID? Run search_user() first!"

Too many overlapping tool definitions consume prompt tokens on every turn and trigger hallucinations. Ruthlessly simplify to one clear tool per job. When a tool fails, return actionable error messages that coach the agent toward recovery rather than raw stack traces.

Key Principle One clear contract per tool. Write errors designed for LLMs (hinting the next logical step) instead of cryptic human exceptions.
SCENE 07 Context Rot

The "Lost in the Middle" Trap

Storyboard Visual An ultra-long desk stretching across the room. The worker can clearly see the note pinned to the front edge and the monitor at the far back edge, but everything piled in the middle is shrouded in thick fog.

A million-token window doesn't mean you should dump a million tokens on the desk. Due to context rot, models suffer degradation when recalling facts buried deep in the middle. Strategic context ordering places system prompts at top and immediate questions at the very end.

Key Principle Assemble context dynamically: filter before ingestion, and position high-priority directives at the primacy and recency edges.
SCENE 08 Future Horizon

Standardized Desk & Orchestration

Storyboard Visual A modular desk equipped with universal MCP snap-on ports. Autonomous drone subagents receive compiled code scripts from the worker and execute micro-tasks across the room in parallel.

The future of agentic architecture is standardization. With Model Context Protocol (MCP) and Agent-to-Agent (A2A) protocols, the desk remains spotless. Plans are compiled into executable code farmed out to parallel subagents, keeping context overhead near zero.

Key Principle "AI's problem was never intelligence. It's what gets put on the desk." Master the desk, and you master the agent.
